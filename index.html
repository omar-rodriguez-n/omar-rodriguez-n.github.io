Neural-network control of an omnidirectional robot

RoboMaster S1 with VICON motion capture. Intelligent Control course, IBERO, fall 2026. Team of five.

My part
  • Wrote the synchronized data acquisition in Python: VICON read at 100 Hz in a parallel thread, the robot commanded at 20 Hz, and a six-phase excitation protocol (calibration, sync marks, asterisk, spiral, filtered noise) inside a safety geofence. About 530 lines.
  • Recorded a 4,344-sample dataset, about 218 s of testing.
  • Checked data quality: the mounting angle of the VICON marker, a 0.10 s plant delay and the speed limits the robot can actually reach.
  • Identified the system with NARX (nonlinear autoregressive with exogenous inputs) and feedforward neural networks in MATLAB (Matrix Laboratory). Found that an R above 0.998 was inflated by comparing it against a persistence predictor.
Team result
  • A closed-loop neural controller, with PyTorch networks trained by my teammates, drove the robot to a target point with 1.18 cm of error.
  • It followed a 40 cm radius circle with a mean radial error of 2.25 cm (RMSE (root mean square error) 3.06 cm, maximum 8.11 cm).

Environmental monitoring network

Internet of Things course, IBERO.

A Raspberry Pi 5 acts as the server, running an MQTT (Message Queuing Telemetry Transport) broker (Mosquitto), Node-RED and MariaDB on a segmented network configured with NetworkManager and nftables.

An ESP32 node in MicroPython publishes temperature, humidity and pressure from a BME280 sensor, plus light from an LDR (light-dependent resistor). Node-RED rules combine those readings with the Open-Meteo forecast API (application programming interface) and log the data.

Raspberry Pi 5, ESP32, MicroPython, Mosquitto, Node-RED, MariaDB, nftables

Code and setup

Wall-following robot built in digital logic

Nexys A7-100T board (Artix-7).

An autonomous robot written in VHDL (Very High Speed Integrated Circuit Hardware Description Language) for an FPGA (field-programmable gate array). It drives four DC (direct current) motors through L298N and DBH-12V H-bridges, follows the left wall at 20 cm with two ultrasonic sensors and turns right at obstacles.

Key fixes: input synchronizers against metastability, corrected distance scaling, a valid signal per sensor, filters over consecutive readings and PWM (pulse-width modulation) speed zones. It boots on its own from SPI (Serial Peripheral Interface) flash.

VHDL, Vivado, Nexys A7-100T, HY-SRF05, HC-SR04
